## Directly relevant research

- [Cross-Lingual Transfer of Evil Vectors Is Directionally Asymmetric](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17880) (2026-10-13) — Tests cross-language transfer of activation directions that induce malicious assistant behavior.
  Scope: Directly manipulates a safety-relevant trait representation and measures behavioral transfer and capability retention across languages and models.
  affected_traits: antisocial_traits. Targets malicious intent and harmful expression, operationalized by two judges' Evil Expression Scores.
  evidence_type: controlled_intervention_experiment, behavioral_evaluation, mechanistic_analysis. Intervenes on residual activations, scores held-out responses and compares vector geometry; cosine alignment is descriptive evidence alongside causal steering tests.
  intervention_type: activation_steering, prompting. Contrastive prompts generate extraction responses; their mean residual-activation difference is added at inference with coefficient 3.0, without weight updates.
  persistence_and_generalization: across_domains_or_languages, across_models, across_prompts_or_tasks. Tests four languages and four instruction-tuned models on 100 held-out questions; transfer is across languages within each model, not vector transfer between models.
  representation_and_mechanism: trait_direction_or_feature. Mean activation differences encode the targeted trait; high-resource vectors align closely, while low-resource extraction produces weakly aligned, less effective directions.
  safety_consequence: other_specified, accuracy_or_capability. High-resource vectors raise malicious-expression scores on low-resource inputs versus ineffective low-resource vectors; steered MMLU stays close to unsteered baseline, without a defense test.
  Evidence: [full-text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17880); review: bootstrap-code-agent.
